Fall detection system consists of: sensors, processing unit, and alarm communication mechanism. In our case, we consider a non-wearable system. Vision-based systems typically use: RGB cameras, thermal cameras, and depth sensors such as Kinect. The most common disadvantages of camera-based systems are: sensitivity to lighting conditions, privacy concerns, and large amounts of processed data.

Human motion is usually divided into: fall, walking, sitting, and other activities of daily living (ADL).

Main stages of the system:
Data Acquisition → Preprocessing (noise filtering, normalization, signal segmentation into time windows) → Feature Extraction → Classification.

Commonly used algorithms: SVM, KNN, Random Forest (RF), Gradient Boosting; however, CNN + LSTM is used most frequently (LSTM analyzes temporal changes). It is often combined with YOLO and a simple program-based classifier.

Potential problems and issues observed in SisFall and KFall datasets:
- excessive generalization (recordings mainly involve young subjects),
- person-dependent validation (the model may learn the individual rather than the fall phenomenon),
- young participants usually exaggerate fall dynamics,
- high percentage of false alarms,
- simple feature extraction may not be sufficient,
- limited evaluation metrics reported by authors (missing F1-score, precision, AUC, and exact number of false alarms),
- after quantization, there is also no information about runtime performance, energy consumption, and real-time stability.

Recommended recording duration: approximately 3–6 seconds.
If YOLO + pose classification is used, recordings should be longer and captured at 30 FPS.
It is necessary to consider gender, weight, age, and height of recorded participants.
